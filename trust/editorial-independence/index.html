TRUST & EDITORIAL RESPONSIBILITY

Editorial Independence

SalimGPT is an independent, research-based documentary media brand. Editorial independence means that research conclusions, factual judgments, documentary framing, corrections and publication decisions should be determined through editorial review rather than inappropriate outside influence.

Independence does not mean working without sources, experts, platforms, collaborators or technology. It means those relationships should not replace the human editorial responsibility behind the final published work.

WHAT INDEPENDENCE MEANS

Editorial decisions remain separate from inappropriate influence

Independence applies across topic selection, research, sourcing, production, publication and correction rather than only at the final editing stage.

Topic Selection

Documentary subjects should be selected according to editorial purpose, research value and audience relevance rather than because an outside party demands a particular conclusion.

Research Conclusions

Evidence should guide factual conclusions. A source, collaborator or interested party should not be able to determine what the research must conclude.

Source Relationships

Access to a source or expert does not give that source editorial control over the documentary or guarantee adoption of their interpretation.

Final Editorial Approval

Final responsibility for factual review, framing, creative direction and publication remains with human editorial decision-making.

Distribution Platforms

Platform features, formats and technical requirements can affect distribution, but they should not determine SalimGPT's factual conclusions.

Corrections

A correction should be based on evidence showing that published material requires change, not simply on pressure to remove an inconvenient but supported fact.

CORE PRINCIPLE

Access, assistance or collaboration does not equal editorial control

Documentary production can involve interviews, archives, experts, researchers, technology providers, distribution platforms and other external resources. Their participation may contribute information or production capability without transferring control of SalimGPT's editorial conclusions.

Where a relationship creates a material conflict, limitation or disclosure issue, the relevant concern should be considered as part of editorial review and transparency.

EDITORIAL DECISION-MAKING

Evidence should be evaluated before interests or preferences

Editorial independence is strongest when factual decisions can be traced back to evidence, source quality, context and review rather than to the preferences of people or organizations affected by a documentary.

Sources can challenge evidence, provide additional records or identify inaccuracies. Those contributions can improve a documentary without giving the source authority over the final editorial judgment.

EDITORIAL SAFEGUARDS

Practices that support independent judgment

No single safeguard resolves every editorial conflict. These principles work together to protect evidence-based decision-making.

01

Verify important claims

Material factual claims should be checked against appropriate evidence rather than accepted because a preferred source states them.

02

Separate fact from interpretation

Documentary framing should distinguish established information from analysis, allegation, inference or unresolved questions.

03

Review relevant conflicts

Material relationships or interests that could affect editorial judgment should be considered during review.

04

Preserve human final approval

Technology and AI tools can assist production, but final editorial responsibility remains human-led.

05

Correct based on evidence

Published work should be updated when review establishes a material error or justified need for clarification.

06

Use transparent distinctions

Sponsorship, partnership, source access, production assistance and editorial approval should not be treated as interchangeable concepts.

OUTSIDE INFLUENCE

External involvement should not predetermine editorial conclusions

Outside organizations and individuals can provide information, access, technical services or other forms of assistance. Editorial independence depends on preserving a clear boundary around final judgment.

Sources & Interviewees

Sources may explain their position, provide documents and challenge inaccuracies, but they do not automatically receive control over the final documentary.

Commercial Relationships

A commercial or promotional relationship, if one exists in a particular context, should not purchase control over factual findings or editorial conclusions.

Partners & Contributors

Collaboration may contribute expertise or production capability while leaving editorial approval with SalimGPT's human-led process.

Platforms & Technology Providers

Technical infrastructure can shape format or distribution, but technology providers should not determine documentary findings.

AI & EDITORIAL CONTROL

AI assistance does not hold editorial authority

SalimGPT may use AI tools to assist research, drafting, organization, visual development, localization, synthetic voice or other production tasks. Tool output can support a workflow, but it is not treated as an independent editorial decision-maker.

Human review remains responsible for assessing evidence, correcting errors, determining context, making creative choices and approving final publication.

DISAGREEMENT & REVIEW

Editorial independence does not mean ignoring criticism

A challenge to published work should be evaluated on its substance. Credible new evidence may justify correction or clarification, while unsupported pressure alone does not establish that the work is inaccurate.

IMPORTANT CONTEXT

Independence is not the same as isolation

Independent documentary work can still rely on experts, primary records, public institutions, archives, witnesses, researchers, translation, technical services and distribution platforms. Independence concerns who controls the editorial judgment, not whether external information or services are ever used.

It also does not mean refusing legitimate criticism. Evidence that identifies an error should be examined through the same editorial standards used during publication.
