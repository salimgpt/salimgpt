HOW WE WORK

Research Methodology

SalimGPT treats research as the foundation of documentary development. The process begins with a defined question, expands through source discovery and evidence assessment, and remains subject to human editorial review before publication.

Research methodology can vary by subject. The purpose is not to force every documentary through one rigid formula, but to apply a consistent standard of evidence-aware, transparent and human-reviewed research.

PURPOSE OF RESEARCH

Research should reduce uncertainty before the story is built

Documentary research is used to understand what is known, what remains uncertain, which claims require verification and what context audiences need before a narrative is developed.

The aim is not to collect information indiscriminately. Research should help identify reliable evidence, competing explanations, factual gaps, relevant chronology and the limits of what the available material can support.

This helps the editorial process distinguish between a strong documentary finding, a reasonable interpretation, an unresolved question and a claim that should not be presented as established fact.

RESEARCH PRINCIPLE Ask → Discover → Assess → Cross-Check → Contextualize → Review

The sequence can change by subject, but evidence should remain connected to the claims it is used to support.

RESEARCH WORKFLOW

Seven stages support the research process

The exact depth of each stage depends on the documentary subject, the available evidence and the level of factual complexity involved.

01

Define the Question

Clarify what the documentary is trying to understand, explain, investigate or verify before gathering large amounts of material.

02

Map the Subject

Identify important people, institutions, events, terminology, chronology, disputes and related areas that may affect the research.

03

Discover Sources

Locate relevant primary and secondary material, public records, research, datasets, institutional documents and other useful evidence where available.

04

Assess Source Quality

Consider authorship, origin, proximity to the claim, methodology, publication context, supporting evidence, limitations and possible conflicts.

05

Cross-Check Important Claims

Compare significant factual claims with additional evidence where possible instead of relying on a single unexamined source.

06

Build Context

Examine chronology, scale, definitions, historical background and other information needed to interpret the evidence accurately.

07

Prepare for Editorial Review

Organize the evidence so major claims, uncertainties and source limitations can be examined during script and publication review.

SOURCE DISCOVERY

Different questions require different kinds of evidence

No single source type is automatically sufficient for every documentary. The useful combination depends on the nature of the claim being researched.

A historical documentary may rely heavily on archival or contemporary records. A scientific subject may require research literature, technical reports and expert context. An investigation may depend on records, first-hand material, corroboration and careful chronology.

Source type alone does not determine reliability. Individual sources still require assessment within their specific context.

Primary Material

Original records, direct documentation, first-hand material or sources close to the event or subject.

Secondary Analysis

Research, reporting, books, reviews or analysis that interpret, synthesize or contextualize primary evidence.

Data & Statistics

Structured numerical evidence that can help establish scale, change, comparison or patterns when the data are relevant and interpretable.

Institutional Material

Official publications, public records, technical documents and other material produced by relevant institutions.

SOURCE ASSESSMENT

A source is evaluated for what it can actually support

Research quality depends not only on finding sources but on understanding their relevance and limitations. A credible source can still be unsuitable for a particular claim if it addresses a different population, period, geography or question.

SalimGPT’s research approach therefore considers the relationship between a source and the exact claim being developed.

Origin Who created the material, and where did it come from?
Relevance Does it directly address the claim or only a related issue?
Method How was the information, evidence or data produced?
Limitations What does the source not establish?

CROSS-CHECKING

Important claims should not depend on unexamined repetition

Multiple websites repeating the same statement do not necessarily represent multiple independent confirmations. Research should consider whether sources are genuinely independent and where the underlying information originated.

Trace the Claim

Identify whether repeated statements point back to an original document, study, interview, record or other underlying source.

Compare Independent Evidence

Look for separate sources that can support, contradict or clarify important factual claims.

Examine Differences

When credible sources disagree, investigate the reason for the difference rather than selecting one account without context.

Preserve Uncertainty

If available evidence cannot resolve a disagreement, the documentary should not manufacture certainty.

CONTEXT MATTERS

A correct fact can still mislead when important context is missing

Dates, geography, definitions, sample size, comparison groups, historical background, scale and surrounding events can materially change how evidence should be interpreted.

Research therefore examines not only whether a statement is technically accurate, but whether the way it is used in the documentary preserves its relevant meaning.

UNCERTAINTY & LIMITATIONS

Research should identify what is not established

Documentary research does not always produce a complete or definitive answer. Records can be missing, data can have limitations, accounts can conflict and some questions may remain unresolved.

Those limitations are part of the evidence environment. They should influence the wording, confidence and framing of the documentary rather than being hidden by stronger language.

01

Avoid presenting inference as documented fact.

02

Identify important gaps when they affect a conclusion.

03

Distinguish disagreement from simple lack of evidence.

04

Adjust certainty to match what the evidence supports.

AI-ASSISTED RESEARCH

AI can assist research tasks, but generated output is not treated as evidence by itself

Artificial intelligence may help organize questions, identify search directions, summarize working material, compare text, support language tasks or assist other parts of the research workflow.

AI output can contain errors, missing context, unsupported claims or incorrect citations. Information produced by an AI system therefore requires verification against appropriate sources before it can support documentary claims.

RESEARCH ORGANIZATION

Evidence should remain traceable during editorial development

Research organization helps the editorial team understand where important claims originated and what material supports or challenges them.

Source Notes

Keep enough source information to reconnect important research findings with the material from which they were derived.

Claim Mapping

Connect significant documentary claims with the evidence that supports, limits or contradicts them.

Chronology

Organize important dates and sequences when timing is necessary to understand cause, development or context.

Review Flags

Mark uncertain, disputed or high-consequence claims for additional human review before publication.

FROM RESEARCH TO FACT-CHECKING

Research creates the evidence base; review tests how it is used

A source can be correctly collected during research and still be misrepresented later in a script, translation, graphic or narration. Fact-checking therefore reviews not only the presence of evidence but also whether the final claim accurately reflects it.

METHODOLOGY IS SUBJECT-SENSITIVE

Not every documentary requires the same research tools

The evidence available for history, science, society, technology, investigations or current affairs can differ substantially. A rigid checklist cannot replace editorial judgment about which research methods are appropriate to a particular subject.

SalimGPT therefore uses a principles-based methodology: define the question, seek relevant evidence, assess sources, preserve uncertainty, provide context and subject important claims to human review.
