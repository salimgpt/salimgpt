HOW WE WORK

Editorial Standards

SalimGPT’s editorial standards provide a framework for turning research, evidence and creative production into documentary work that remains subject to human judgment, review and final approval.

These standards are principles for editorial decision-making, not a claim that every documentary subject can be handled through one identical process. The depth and form of review can vary according to the evidence, subject and potential consequences of publication.

EDITORIAL PURPOSE

Editorial standards connect evidence with publication decisions

Research can identify evidence, and fact-checking can test individual claims, but documentary publication also requires broader editorial judgment.

Editors must consider whether information is relevant, properly contextualized, proportionate to the evidence, clearly attributed and presented in a way that does not create a misleading impression.

SalimGPT uses editorial review to bring those decisions together before final publication approval.

EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE Evidence → Context → Judgment → Review → Approval → Accountability

Technology can support the process, but final editorial responsibility remains human-led.

CORE STANDARDS

Eight principles guide editorial review

These principles help determine how documentary material should be researched, written, presented, reviewed and corrected.

01

Accuracy

Factual claims should be supported by appropriate evidence and should not be presented with greater certainty than that evidence allows.

02

Evidence

Important claims should remain connected to relevant sources whose origin, limitations and relationship to the claim can be examined.

03

Context

Facts should be presented with the chronology, definitions, scale or surrounding information needed to understand them fairly.

04

Attribution

Allegations, interpretations, opinions and source-specific claims should be attributed when attribution is necessary to understand who is making the statement.

05

Proportion

Weak, unusual or poorly supported evidence should not be given more editorial weight than its significance justifies.

06

Transparency

Relevant limitations, production methods, synthetic media use or other information can be disclosed when audiences need that context to understand the work.

07

Human Review

Research, scripts, visuals and AI-assisted outputs remain subject to human editorial judgment before final publication.

08

Correction

Published work can be reviewed and corrected when substantive factual or contextual problems are identified.

ACCURACY & CONTEXT

A fact can be technically correct and still be misleading

Editorial review considers more than whether individual words or numbers are correct. It also examines the meaning created when information is selected, ordered, summarized or placed beside other material.

Missing context can change how audiences interpret a claim. Dates, geographic scope, definitions, comparison groups, historical background or evidentiary limitations may therefore be necessary for accuracy.

When a fuller context materially changes the meaning of a statement, that context should be considered during editorial review.

CLEAR DISTINCTIONS

Fact, interpretation and attributed viewpoint should not be blurred

Documentary storytelling can include analysis and interpretation, but audiences should not be led to believe that every interpretation is an independently established fact.

Documented Fact

A factual statement supported by evidence appropriate to the claim being made.

Interpretation

A conclusion or explanation derived from evidence but not identical to the evidence itself.

Attributed Viewpoint

A claim, assessment, allegation or opinion clearly identified as belonging to a particular source.

Unresolved Question

A point where the available evidence does not justify a definitive conclusion.

FAIRNESS & PROPORTION

Editorial balance is not the same as giving every claim equal weight

Documentary fairness requires relevant viewpoints, evidence and uncertainty to be represented in proportion to their significance and support.

A weak or unsupported claim should not automatically receive the same emphasis as a well-supported finding merely because the two positions disagree.

At the same time, credible disagreement should not be hidden when it is material to understanding the subject.

01
Evidence Strength

Give editorial weight according to what the available evidence can support.

02
Relevant Disagreement

Include credible disagreement when it materially affects interpretation.

03
Avoid False Equivalence

Do not imply equal evidentiary support when the available evidence is substantially different.

04
Preserve Uncertainty

Where evidence remains unresolved, avoid presenting a stronger conclusion than review supports.

LANGUAGE & FRAMING

Headlines, captions and narration should not overstate the evidence

Editorial accuracy applies to presentation as well as body text. A documentary can contain carefully sourced research while still creating a misleading impression through exaggerated titles, captions or narration.

Language should therefore reflect the strength, scope and certainty of the available evidence.

HIGH-CONSEQUENCE MATERIAL

Some subjects require additional editorial care

Serious allegations, personal reputation, health, safety, legal matters, investigations, public affairs and other high-consequence subjects can require stronger source review, clearer attribution and more precise wording.

The potential consequence of publication does not decide whether a fact is true, but it can affect how carefully the evidence and presentation should be reviewed.

VISUAL EDITORIAL STANDARDS

Visual choices should not create a false factual impression

Images, graphics, reconstructions, archival material and synthetic media can communicate factual meaning even when no explicit factual statement appears on screen.

Context

A visual should not be presented as evidence of a different event, location, person or period.

Captions

Captions and labels should accurately describe what a visual shows and avoid unsupported interpretation.

Alteration

Editing or compositing should not materially misrepresent what the original visual evidence establishes.

Synthetic Media

AI-generated or reconstructed visuals can require additional context when audiences might otherwise mistake them for direct documentary evidence.

HUMAN-LED, AI-ASSISTED

AI assistance does not transfer editorial authority to an automated system

AI may assist selected research, drafting, visual, language, localization or production tasks where useful. Those outputs can still contain errors, unsupported claims, misleading summaries or fabricated information.

Editorial judgment, source verification, fact-checking, creative direction, review and final publication approval therefore remain human responsibilities.

MULTILINGUAL EDITORIAL REVIEW

Translation should preserve evidence, meaning and attribution

A multilingual documentary should not become stronger, weaker or materially different merely because it has been adapted into another language.

Review therefore considers whether important factual meaning, uncertainty, attribution and contextual distinctions have been preserved through translation and localization.

EDITORIAL INDEPENDENCE

Editorial conclusions should follow evidence and judgment rather than outside pressure

SalimGPT’s editorial process is intended to separate documentary judgment from external pressure that would require evidence to be distorted, suppressed or overstated.

Editorial independence does not eliminate the need for criticism, correction or accountability. It means those decisions should be made through editorial review rather than by allowing outside interests to determine factual conclusions.

BEFORE PUBLICATION

Final review asks whether the documentary is ready to publish

Different projects can require different levels of review, but the publication decision should consider more than production completion alone.

01

Research Complete Enough?

Determine whether important evidence gaps remain that materially affect the documentary.

02

Claims Properly Supported?

Confirm that significant factual statements remain traceable to appropriate evidence.

03

Context Preserved?

Check whether editing, summarization or narration has removed information necessary for fair interpretation.

04

Attribution Clear?

Ensure contested claims, opinions and allegations are attributed where necessary.

05

Visuals Appropriate?

Review whether graphics, archival material or synthetic media create an accurate impression.

06

Human Approval Complete?

Confirm that the relevant human editorial review is complete before publication.

AFTER PUBLICATION

Editorial responsibility continues after a documentary is published

New evidence, factual mistakes, context problems or production issues can emerge after publication. A completed documentary is therefore not treated as beyond further review.

Substantive issues can be examined through SalimGPT’s corrections and updates process, and audiences can use the correction request route to submit relevant information for review.
