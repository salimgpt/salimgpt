HOW WE WORK

Fact-Checking

SalimGPT uses human-led fact-checking to examine how research and evidence are represented in documentary scripts, narration, graphics, captions, translations and other published elements.

Fact-checking is not limited to asking whether a source exists. It also examines whether the claim accurately reflects that source, whether important context is preserved and whether the level of certainty is justified.

PURPOSE

Fact-checking tests the documentary against its evidence

Research creates the factual foundation of a documentary. Fact-checking examines whether the material produced from that research still matches the evidence once it has been written, edited, translated, narrated or visualized.

A source can be collected correctly and later be overstated, quoted inaccurately, placed in the wrong context or used to support a broader claim than the evidence allows.

The purpose of fact-checking is therefore to reconnect important documentary claims with the evidence behind them before final publication approval.

REVIEW PRINCIPLE Claim → Evidence → Context → Wording → Presentation → Approval

A claim should remain accurate not only in isolation, but also in the way it is presented to the audience.

WHAT WE CHECK

Seven areas can require verification

The depth of review depends on the documentary subject, the type of claim and the potential consequence of getting the information wrong.

01

Factual Claims

Names, dates, events, places, relationships, quotations and other factual statements are checked against appropriate evidence.

02

Source Matching

Important claims are reviewed to determine whether the cited or underlying source actually supports the wording used in the documentary.

03

Numbers & Statistics

Numerical claims are checked for definitions, denominators, dates, units, comparisons and other context needed for accurate interpretation.

04

Attribution

Statements, allegations, interpretations and opinions are reviewed to ensure they are attributed where attribution is necessary.

05

Context

The review considers whether technically correct information could become misleading when separated from relevant chronology, geography or surrounding facts.

06

Visual Claims

Images, graphics, captions and synthetic or altered media are reviewed for whether they could create a misleading factual impression.

07

Translation & Localization

Multilingual versions are reviewed for changes in factual meaning, certainty, attribution or context that may have occurred during adaptation.

REVIEW WORKFLOW

A claim moves through a verification sequence

The sequence can vary, but significant claims should remain traceable to evidence and receive human review before publication.

1
IDENTIFY

Locate the claim that needs checking

Identify factual statements, numbers, quotations, visual implications or other elements where accuracy depends on external evidence.

2
TRACE

Connect the claim to its source

Return to the relevant record, study, document, dataset, interview or other material supporting the statement.

3
COMPARE

Compare the published wording with the evidence

Check whether the documentary says more, less or something materially different from what the source establishes.

4
CONTEXT

Review surrounding facts and limitations

Examine dates, geography, definitions, competing evidence, uncertainty and other information that may affect interpretation.

5
CORRECT

Revise wording or presentation where necessary

Adjust inaccurate, overstated, ambiguous or unsupported material before publication.

6
REVIEW

Confirm the revised material

Re-check significant changes to ensure that the final version still reflects the evidence accurately.

7
APPROVE

Keep final approval human-led

Publication proceeds only after the relevant editorial review is complete and a human decision is made to approve the material.

CLAIM-TO-EVIDENCE REVIEW

The source must support the claim at the level it is presented

A documentary can misrepresent evidence even when the source itself is credible. A narrow finding can become misleading if it is rewritten as a universal conclusion, or an uncertain estimate can become inaccurate if it is presented as an established fact.

Fact-checking therefore compares the exact wording, scope and confidence of the documentary claim with the evidence behind it.

Scope Does the wording extend beyond what the evidence covers?
Certainty Is the level of confidence justified?
Meaning Does the statement preserve the source’s actual meaning?
Context Is information missing that would materially change interpretation?

NUMBERS & STATISTICS

Numerical precision does not guarantee interpretive accuracy

A number can be copied correctly and still become misleading if its unit, denominator, time period, population or comparison basis is omitted.

Numerical fact-checking therefore considers both the arithmetic value and the context required to understand what that value means.

01
Units

Confirm whether the measurement is expressed in the correct unit.

02
Denominator

Verify what total or population a percentage represents.

03
Time

Confirm the period to which a figure applies.

04
Comparison

Check whether compared figures were produced on a compatible basis.

ATTRIBUTION CHECK

The documentary should distinguish its own factual statements from attributed claims

Some material reflects what a person, organization, witness, researcher, official source or other actor has said rather than an independently established conclusion.

Fact-checking reviews whether attribution is preserved when needed so audiences can understand who is making a particular claim, interpretation, allegation or estimate.

VISUAL VERIFICATION

Images and graphics can make factual claims without using words

Visual material can imply identity, location, chronology, scale, causation or authenticity. Those implications may require review just as written statements do.

Image Context

Check whether a visual actually relates to the event, place, person or period it appears to represent.

Captions

Review captions and labels so they do not assign an unsupported meaning to an otherwise authentic image.

Alteration

Consider whether editing, compositing or other modifications materially change the factual impression created by the visual.

Synthetic Media

Review AI-generated or synthetic material for whether audiences need additional context about what is real, reconstructed or illustrative.

MULTILINGUAL FACT-CHECKING

Translation should preserve factual meaning, not only vocabulary

A documentary can be accurate in one language and become misleading in another if translation changes certainty, attribution, technical meaning, chronology or the scope of a claim.

Multilingual review therefore considers whether the adapted version continues to represent the underlying evidence accurately.

AI-ASSISTED REVIEW

AI can assist checking tasks, but it is not the final verifier

AI tools may assist with comparison, transcription, language review, consistency checks, research organization or identifying material that requires closer attention.

AI-generated output can itself contain errors or fabricated information. SalimGPT therefore does not treat an AI response as independent proof that a documentary claim is correct.

HIGH-CONSEQUENCE MATERIAL

Some claims require additional verification and more careful wording

Material involving serious allegations, personal reputation, health, safety, legal matters, public affairs or other high-consequence subjects can require stronger evidentiary support and closer editorial review.

Where available evidence remains limited, disputed or uncertain, the final wording should preserve those limitations rather than presenting unsupported certainty.

HUMAN REVIEW

Fact-checking supports editorial judgment; it does not replace it

A factual review can identify whether claims match evidence, but publication also requires decisions about relevance, proportion, narrative structure, clarity, ethics and presentation.

Final responsibility therefore remains with the human editorial process rather than being delegated to an automated checking system.

AFTER PUBLICATION

Fact-checking does not mean published work can never require correction

New evidence can emerge, source material can be updated and mistakes can still be identified after publication. When a substantive issue is raised, the published material can require additional review.

SalimGPT separates pre-publication fact-checking from its corrections and updates process so post-publication issues can be examined through a defined editorial route.
