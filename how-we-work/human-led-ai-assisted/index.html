HOW WE WORK

Human-Led, AI-Assisted

SalimGPT uses artificial intelligence as an assistant within selected parts of documentary production. AI can help organize information, develop drafts, support language work, create production assets and accelerate repetitive tasks.

AI does not hold editorial authority at SalimGPT. Source verification, fact-checking, interpretation, creative direction, editorial judgment, review and final publication approval remain human-led.

THE MODEL

AI supports the workflow; people remain responsible for the documentary

SalimGPT treats AI as a production capability rather than an editorial decision-maker. The distinction matters because AI systems can generate useful material while also producing errors, unsupported claims, invented details or misleading simplifications.

Human oversight is therefore built around the questions that matter most: What is true? What evidence supports it? What context is necessary? How should it be interpreted? How should it be presented? Is the final documentary ready to publish?

Those decisions are not delegated to an automated system.

RESPONSIBILITY MODEL AI Assistance → Human Verification → Human Judgment → Human Approval

Technology may contribute to the process, but editorial accountability remains with people.

06

Consistency Checks

AI can help identify wording differences, possible inconsistencies or material that may require closer editorial attention.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITY

The most important editorial decisions remain human

AI can generate or transform material quickly, but speed does not establish accuracy, relevance or editorial value.

SalimGPT therefore keeps critical decisions under human control throughout the documentary workflow.

Verification Check important information against appropriate sources.
Interpretation Decide what the evidence means and where uncertainty remains.
Creative Direction Determine narrative, visual and editorial choices.
Final Approval Make the human decision to publish the finished work.

AI & EVIDENCE

AI output is not treated as independent documentary evidence

A generated answer, summary or citation can be useful as a starting point for research, but it does not become evidence merely because an AI system produced it.

Important factual claims need to be verified against appropriate underlying sources. Where AI suggests a source, quotation, number or factual detail, that material should be checked before it is relied upon in a documentary.

WHY HUMAN REVIEW MATTERS

AI assistance introduces risks that require editorial review

Useful output and unreliable output can look equally polished. Human review is therefore necessary even when AI appears confident or fluent.

Fabricated Information

AI can generate names, quotations, citations or details that appear plausible but are not supported by real evidence.

Missing Context

Summaries can omit limitations, chronology, definitions or other information necessary for accurate interpretation.

Overconfident Wording

Generated text can present uncertain, disputed or limited information more confidently than the evidence allows.

Inconsistent Output

Similar prompts can produce different answers, requiring human comparison rather than automatic acceptance.

Misleading Transformation

Translation, rewriting or visual generation can alter factual meaning even when the original material was accurate.

False Sense of Authority

Fluent presentation can make unsupported material appear more authoritative than it actually is.

HUMAN CHECKPOINTS

Human review is built into the workflow

AI-assisted material can move forward only through the editorial process, where its relevance, factual accuracy and presentation are evaluated.

01

Before Use

Decide whether AI assistance is appropriate for the task and what human review will be required.

02

During Research

Verify factual suggestions, citations and source claims against appropriate underlying material.

03

During Writing

Review generated or AI-assisted text for accuracy, attribution, context and unsupported conclusions.

04

During Visual Production

Evaluate whether synthetic or AI-assisted visuals could create a false impression about what is real or documented.

05

During Localization

Check whether translation or adaptation preserves factual meaning, terminology and certainty.

06

Before Publication

Complete human editorial review and make the final decision about whether the documentary is ready to publish.

VISUAL & SYNTHETIC MEDIA

AI-generated visuals remain subject to editorial context and creative direction

Synthetic media can support illustration, reconstruction, explanation or visual development. Its use should not cause audiences to mistake generated material for direct documentary evidence when that distinction matters.

Human editorial review considers how the visual is used, what factual impression it creates and whether additional context is necessary.

CREATIVE DIRECTION

Using AI does not replace original editorial and creative direction

Tools can generate options, but the documentary still requires human decisions about subject, structure, narrative emphasis, visual language, pacing, tone and final form.

SalimGPT’s creative direction is therefore based on human editorial intent, with AI used as one possible production resource within that direction.

MULTILINGUAL WORK

AI can assist language adaptation, but meaning still requires review

Translation tools can accelerate multilingual production, yet translation is not purely mechanical. Terminology, attribution, tone, factual certainty and cultural context can change meaning across languages.

Human review therefore remains important when AI assists translation or localization.

TRANSPARENCY

Relevant AI use can require explanation when it affects how audiences understand the work

Not every routine production tool requires a separate disclosure. However, AI or synthetic media use can become editorially significant when it materially affects how audiences understand authenticity, reconstruction, evidence or the origin of a visual or audio element.

In those situations, additional context can help distinguish documentary evidence from generated, reconstructed or illustrative material.

01
Authenticity

Could audiences mistake generated material for an authentic record?

02
Reconstruction

Is a generated scene illustrating something that was not directly recorded?

03
Evidence

Could a synthetic element be mistaken for factual source material?

04
Audience Context

Would additional explanation materially improve understanding?

FINAL STANDARD

Human accountability is the boundary that defines the model

SalimGPT may use advanced AI tools extensively where they are useful, but the presence of AI does not change who is responsible for editorial decisions.

Human review remains responsible for deciding what is accepted, rejected, revised, attributed, corrected and ultimately published.
